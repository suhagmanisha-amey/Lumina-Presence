## 1. The AI-driven middle-management squeeze in Indian tech — STRONGEST

**What it is:** A live, compounding story. Oracle cut 1,000+ Indian tech jobs in a fresh round roughly four weeks ago (https://www.deccanherald.com/business/companies/oracle-begins-fresh-round-of-layoffs-over-1000-indian-employees-set-to-lose-jobs-4147165). A layoff/downsizing tracker was updated as recently as yesterday (https://intellizence.com/insights/layoff-downsizing/major-companies-that-announced-mass-layoffs/), alongside an India-specific tracker (https://indialayoffs.com/). Around this sit analyses of "silent layoffs" putting 25–35k Indian IT roles at risk (https://www.ownyourcareer.in/blog/silent-layoffs-india-it-2026-35000-jobs-ai-automation-risk), a piece specifically on middle-management layoffs in India (https://sandeepanand.in/career-coaching/blogs-middle-management-layoffs-india-2026/), and a Forbes India argument that AI is erasing entry-level work and creating a **management vacuum** (https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/thought-leadership/great-lakes-institute-of-management/ai-disruption-erases-entry-level-jobs-creates-india-management-vacuum/2994794/1). A widely-cited projection holds that half of middle managers could be displaced by 2027 (https://shawnkanungo.com/blog/why-50-of-middle-managers-could-be-replaced-by-ai-by-2027-and-what-to-do-if-youre-one).

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** Lumina's buyer is the competent-but-invisible manager. In a stable org, invisibility costs you a promotion. In a thinning-out org, invisibility costs you the job — because the person who decides who stays is working off an *impression* of indispensability formed in meetings, not off your output file. The Forbes India "management vacuum" framing is the gift here: layers are being removed *and* capable leaders are scarce at the same time. That is precisely the gap the Lumina Method claims to close — the difference between being a good operator and being read as command-authority.

**Why this week and not any week:** The Oracle round is ~4 weeks old and the trackers are updating daily, which means this is in the feed *now*, not as a 2026 think-piece but as named-company news. It also lands in the window where Indian IT reports quarterly results and headcount, so the "where did the managers go" conversation is at peak volume.

**Content angles:** "AI can do your reporting. It can't do your presence." / The three meeting behaviours that make you look replaceable. / Why restructures spare the person leadership can picture in the room.

## 2. Senior hiring has flipped to fitment over speed — and boards have stopped being impressed by résumés

**What it is:** This is the one batch I read in full. Taggd's India hiring research finds a sharp bifurcation: roughly 80% of hiring leaders prioritise *speed over job-fitment* for frontline roles, while about 90% reverse that priority for senior positions (https://taggd.in/blogs/hiring-outlook-in-india-insights-from-india-decoding-jobs-report/). Alongside it: ManpowerGroup's 2026 Talent Shortage Survey reports 82% of Indian employers struggling to fill roles versus ~72% globally (https://www.thepeoplesboard.com/recruitment/hiring-in-india-2026-guide/); executive-search commentary notes CXO compensation in India grew 18–25% YoY, the fastest in a decade, against the widest supply-demand gap yet for senior talent (https://wsneconsulting.com/guides/leadership-hiring-trends-2026); and a CEO-hiring piece states plainly that boards and founders are no longer impressed by long résumés alone (https://www.corporatestalwarts.com/ceo-hiring-trends-india-2026-leadership-hiring-insights/). There's also a credible outside-India piece on the problem of *measuring* executive presence (https://www.staffingadvisors.com/blog/measuring-the-intangible-executive-presence/).

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** This is statistical proof of the exact wound Lumina addresses. Your audience's private theory is "I'm qualified, so why am I overlooked?" The data gives the honest answer: at senior level, the filter stopped being the credential and became the judgement call — and judgement calls are made on bearing, how you hold a room, how you answer the hard question. 90% of senior decisions being fitment-led is the most persuasive single number I found for this brand all week.

**Why it's timely (with a caveat):** This is *cycle*-timely rather than week-timely — the reports are months old. What makes it live right now is that it's the natural counterweight to opportunity #1: the same market cutting managers is paying record premiums for leaders it believes in. Pair them in one week and you have a narrative, not two posts. Be honest in the caption that these are 2026 reports, not breaking news.
