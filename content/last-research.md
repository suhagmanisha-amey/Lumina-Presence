### 1. Six Indian IT majors shed ~24,000 jobs in a year — published today

**What it is:** Staffing firm Xpheno data, reported via the Economic Times and picked up today (23 Oct 2026), shows combined headcount at TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro, Tech Mahindra and LTIMindtree fell nearly 3% — from a base of 822,144. The same roundup notes GCCs are paying an "AI premium" while traditional services shrink.

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** Your core buyer is the qualified-but-overlooked manager — and a large share of India's mid-career managerial population sits inside exactly these six companies or their client ecosystem. When headcount contracts while GCCs pay premiums, the person who gets kept, moved, or poached is not necessarily the most competent one — it's the one leadership can *picture* in the next role. That is the precise gap the Lumina Method sells into: competence that nobody has seen demonstrated in a room.

**Why this week:** The Xpheno/ET numbers dropped into the news cycle today. There is a 48–72 hour window where "24,000 jobs" is the number in everyone's WhatsApp forwards.

**Angle:** "When headcount shrinks, visibility stops being vanity." Carousel on the three moments in a quarter where leadership actually forms an opinion of you — and how most people go silent in all three.

### 2. India GCC leaders report AI adoption is near-universal — but realised productivity gains are under 15%

**What it is:** The Embark report, out today (23 Oct), surveyed 100 India GCC leaders plus executive interviews. Findings: 92% of surveyed leaders say they've moved beyond selective AI adoption, yet realised productivity gains remain below 15%; 71% expect their centres to expand scope through 2031.

**Why it matters to *this* audience:** This is the "competence isn't the bottleneck" story in hard numbers. Everyone has the tool; almost nobody is converting it into visible results. For a manager inside a GCC, the differentiator in 2026 is no longer technical capability — it's the ability to stand in front of a global leadership team and *narrate* judgment, trade-offs and conviction. That's bearing and tone, not tooling. GCCs are also where India's most ambitious mid-career professionals are concentrated, and they're reporting to leadership in another timezone who only ever see them for 30 minutes on a call.

**Why this week:** The report is hours old and will be covered across Indian business media for the next several days.

**Angle:** "Your company has the AI. The gap is who gets believed in the review meeting." Reel on command-authority on a video call — the one place Indian GCC managers are judged most and trained least.

A related finding: only about 12% of workers believe AI has meaningfully changed how work gets done.

**Why it matters to *this* audience:** Your audience *is* the manager layer. This research reframes their private experience — "I'm doing more and being seen less" — as a documented structural condition rather than a personal failing. That's a powerful, non-shaming hook for a coaching brand, and the India companion report lets you localise it rather than recycling a global stat.

**Why this week:** Gallup's main report page was refreshed about 11 days ago and the India report about five weeks ago, so this is "current month" rather than breaking. It stays usable for several weeks — treat it as your anchor stat for the quarter rather than a one-day hook.

**Angle:** "22% of managers are engaged. The other 78% aren't lazy — they're unheard." Lead with Colonel Suhag on what command does differently when morale drops: presence before policy.

**Why it matters to *this* audience:** The old defensive logic — "get senior enough and you're safe" — is visibly failing. Seniority is no longer protection; *being indispensable in the room* is.
