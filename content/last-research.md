### 1. Gallup's 2026 workplace data landed in late September — and the headline is that being a manager has stopped feeling worth it

**What it is:** Gallup's *State of the Global Workplace: 2026* report (posted ~18 days ago) finds global employee engagement fell to 20% in 2025, its lowest since 2020, with an estimated $10 trillion productivity cost. One of its featured sections is framed around "The Shrinking Perk of Being a Manager." Alongside it, Gallup published an India-specific companion, *Rebuilding Indian Workplaces for the Future* (~24 days ago), built on the global data plus an online survey of Institute of Directors (India) members. Gallup's related India analysis reports 59% of India's employees are "Not Engaged" — described as people seeking greater clarity, communication and connection with their manager — with disengagement costing India roughly $351bn a year, about 9% of GDP.

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** This is the Lumina Method's thesis stated in third-party data. The gap Gallup describes isn't a skills gap or an effort gap — it's a **clarity-and-communication gap between a manager and the room**. Your audience is the person on the wrong side of that: competent, delivering, but not landing. "Not Engaged" is also the precise emotional state of someone whose ideas get re-stated by a colleague and credited to them. And the "shrinking perk of being a manager" framing gives you a non-whiny way to talk about why good operators stall — without blaming them.

**Why now:** These reports dropped in the last 3–4 weeks and are the current reference point HR and leadership commentators in India are citing. Credible window to ride it: the next 2–3 weeks, before the Q4 content cycle takes over.

### 2. The 2026 appraisal cycle ran late and ran lean — and the people who lost out are still sitting with it

**What it is:** foundit's Appraisal Survey 2026 (published 24 July 2026) found that appraisals were running late this year, that more than half of professionals surveyed were already job-hunting, and that over one in four respondents (28%) had still not been appraised when fieldwork closed in June. EY India projected average increments around 9.1%, with organisations shifting toward skill-based pay frameworks. A late-August India-IT-focused analysis puts this cycle at 7–8% — described as the leanest since 2020 — against 20–35% for job-switchers.

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** This is the exact wound. A lean cycle means the hike is no longer a reward for output — it's allocated by *visibility and perceived seniority*. The person who was passed over this year wasn't outworked; they were out-positioned in the rooms where the slate was decided. "Skill-based pay" also means you now have to *articulate* your value category, not just deliver it. That's a presence problem, not a performance problem, and that's your product.

**Why now (caveat):** This is the weakest part of the case — the survey is from July. But the *consequence* is live right now: with cycles delayed and Q2 closed, deferred and mid-year conversations are landing in Oct–Nov, and the festive-season promotion/bonus announcements sit right ahead. Treat this as a "six months after you were passed over, here's what to do before the next cycle opens" piece, not as breaking news. Don't present the stats as fresh.

### 4. Hybrid policy in India is consolidating around "come in for the meetings that matter" — which raises the stakes on every in-person hour

**What it is:** An RTO tracker updated ~7 days ago documents the continuing global shift of major employers back to in-office and full-time-in-office policies through 2026. India-specific HR guidance is converging on an *activity-based* model: senior stakeholder meetings and similar high-stakes interactions are specified and scheduled in-person, while routine individual-contributor work stays location-flexible — framed explicitly as turning a culture fight into a workflow decision. There's real resistance in the data, with one India-focused read reporting ~57% of professionals would consider quitting if remote work were removed.

**Why it matters to *this* audience specifically:** If the office is now reserved for senior stakeholder meetings, then **office time is evaluation time**. Your audience no longer has a full week of ambient presence to build a reputation in — they have a compressed set of high-stakes rooms where bearing, tone and silence do all the work. The professional who was invisible on Zoom now gets a second chance in person; the one who coasted on being "always online" loses their cover. This is a structural tailwind for presence coaching and nobody in the space seems to be framing it that way.

**Why now (caveat):** The RTO tracker was refreshed last week, which gives you a live peg; the India policy guidance is a few months older. **Hard avoid:** the India WFH conversation has a political thread running through it (a PM-level appeal on remote work surfaced in several results). Steer entirely clear — talk only about what corporate policy means for individual presence.
