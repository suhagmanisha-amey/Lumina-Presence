## 3. Gallup: Indian manager engagement fell off a cliff — and it's being costed in trillions

**What it is:** The Hans India ran an opinion analysis (~9–10 Oct 2026) on Gallup's latest workplace data. The numbers: only 30% of Indian managers were engaged in 2025, down from 39% in 2024. Individual contributor engagement fell from 24% to 19%. Gallup puts India's disengagement cost at roughly $351 billion / ₹32.7 trillion a year — about 9% of GDP. Gallup identifies managers as the critical link between organisational priorities and daily employee experience.

**Why it matters to this audience specifically:** A nine-point single-year collapse in *manager* engagement is the statistical shape of exactly what Lumina's audience describes — capable people who've stopped putting themselves forward because it stopped producing results. The article's own framing is unusually blunt about the behavioural pattern: do what's necessary, avoid responsibility, escalate difficult decisions, protect yourself. That's not laziness — it's what withdrawal looks like in a manager who's been overlooked long enough. Lumina can name that pattern with compassion and then offer the counter-move.

**Why this week:** The piece is three days old and the Gallup India figure is the number every Indian HR newsletter is passing around right now. A credible ₹32.7 trillion statistic is also the most shareable asset available this week — big enough to stop a scroll, specific enough to be defensible.
